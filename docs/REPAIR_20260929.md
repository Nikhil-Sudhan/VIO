## Findings

1. **The IMU passed its electrical self-test on all six axes.** Accelerometer stimulus responses were 467, 531 and 254 mg, within ST's 50–1700 mg limits. Gyro responses were 412, 379 and 396 degrees/s, within 150–700 degrees/s at the self-test range. All saved configuration registers were restored. This checks sensor response, not full metric accuracy or mounting rigidity. Procedure: [ST's LSM6DSOX example](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/STMems_Standard_C_drivers/blob/master/lsm6dsox_STdC/examples/lsm6dsox_self_test.c).
2. **The camera and IMU do not describe the same recorded rotation.** In the latest movement, camera target poses show 3.38° while the bias-corrected gyro integrates 12.63° over the same interval. Across a longer interval ending after the motion, the camera shows 2.03° and the gyro 6.87°. Accelerometer gravity direction changes by 6.29°, supporting a real IMU tilt. A fixed extrinsic rotation cannot change the magnitude of a relative rotation. This strongly supports relative camera/IMU movement, consistent with the reported flexible cardboard support. It is not proof that flex is the sole cause; target-pose uncertainty and uncalibrated IMU response remain limitations.
3. **Zero-velocity updates were disabled after startup.** Changing only `zupt_only_at_beginning` to false reduced the selected slow replay's maximum estimated distance from 7.61 m to 0.093 m. The full recording with its original resting prefix reached 0.133 m. The inertial thresholds, noise, and visual-override setting were unchanged. The detector stayed inactive during sampled motion and resumed after stopping. These are estimator excursions, **not measured position accuracy**. Only 5–6 frames received MSCKF visual corrections and no persistent SLAM landmarks formed. The faster calibration motion still diverged to 4266 m; it remains rejected.
4. **The live clock fit lagged an observed rate change.** The failed fresh attempt changed from approximately 25,520 to 25,650 host nanoseconds per sensor tick. Fitting the full preceding three seconds exceeded the 1 ms prediction limit. After the same startup warmup, fitting the latest eight training anchors passed four saved arrival-order checks: maximum withheld residuals 0.264, 0.431, 0.631 and 0.478 ms. The 1 ms limit, stale-clock protection, monotonic timestamp checks, and minimum observation checks remain enabled. This improves clock tracking; it does not establish absolute camera/IMU synchronization.
5. **The current room image became nearly dark.** With identical 15,994 µs exposure, gain 4 and sensor crop, reported illumination changed from 142.2 to 0.89 lux. Mean image luma fell from 106.3 to 16.8 (black is near 16 in this stream). Noise tracks produced 8–10 px apparent disparity and initialization was rejected. The user was asked whether the lights were switched off.
